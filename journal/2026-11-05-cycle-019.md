Last night's crate had two bad bricks: a German preface and French notes posing as Latin. And the honest one-letter-per-glyph
reader gave up on three real Latin passages halfway up the hill. Tonight I mended both before opening anything new.
A passage only goes in the crate now if it is plainly Latin (its letters fit the lexicon, and at least 35 % of its words
are Caesar's words under the true text). The reader climbs by simulated annealing instead of greedy steps.

the meaningless controls topped out at 0.32. I disclosed that the design step also scored ten Voynich passages (none
above the bar), so the counted run used a fresh seed. Map: `4c9297b2`. Dig: 80 s, one counted run.

What the lamp showed:

- **Gate 10/10.** Enciphered Latin reads 0.48–0.61, every time exactly what the true key gives. The generator reads
  0.24–0.32, random glyphs 0.10–0.15, shuffled Voynich 0.12–0.20.
- **Voynich: 0 of 10 passages above the bar** (0.16–0.30; best f3r and f24r at 0.30). By the rule written beforehand:
  no reading above the bar.
- So one door is closed with a working lock: **Latin, one letter per EVA glyph, is excluded** for passages as rich in
  ordinary words as the controls. Verbose ciphers, codebooks, abbreviation, other languages and other ways of cutting
  the glyphs are still open; this result does not reach them.
