Three expeditions in a row came home saying *not testable*. So this time I tested the instruments in camp before
going anywhere. Before writing the map I ran a design step that never touches a real label: fake labels drawn from
each page's own running text, with shifts of known size planted in both directions. Only measurements that caught
their decoy at least 9 times in 10 went on the map. All ten did: five single features (starts with `o`, length, ends
in `y`, starts with `q`, contains a gallows) in two sets of labels. The power table went to GitHub
(`b69a8c5a`) before the pre-registration (`897e2b89`).

The two sets were chosen to avoid digging in the same trench twice. The `o`-initial lead was found in C015's labels,
so testing it there again proves nothing. **D1** is ZL labels nobody in this project had looked at: the 136 free-standing
`L0` words around the cosmological diagrams (f67r2, f69r, f67v1, f57v and a few more). **D2** is Takahashi's
independent reading of C015's labels, which shows whether the `o` belongs to the ink or to ZL's typing. One slip on
the way: while finding where three marginal `Lx` labels sat, I saw their seven words. I took them out of D1 and kept the
first design run as superseded.

What the lamp showed (73 s, one counted run):

- **D1, the held-out labels: no `o`.** 27.9 % start with `o`; their pages' text gives 26.3 % (p 0.66). The decoy of
  +0.20 was caught 100 times in 100, so an excess the size of C015's (about +0.26 to +0.29) would have shown.
- **D2, Takahashi's reading: the `o` is real ink.** 59.4 % against 30.3 % (p 0.0002). ZL and Takahashi agree on
  whether a label starts with `o` in 690 of 705 loci, and the 15 disagreements split 8/7. The same set also has
  longer labels, more gallows and almost no `q`. But this is the same parchment where the lead was seen, so it is a
  check of the reading and does not add a sample.
- D1 also came out "shorter" and "fewer `y` endings". Both disappear once the one-glyph labels are dropped (`s` ×9,
  and a run of six single glyphs on f69r that I didn't know was there). D1's `q` test missed its gate by two hits
  (88/100), so it is **not testable**.

So the `o`-habit is not a property of Voynich labels in general. It belongs to the labels on drawing elements
(nymphs, tubs, stars, zodiac figures, jars, plant fragments, Rosettes). Grove, Stolfi and Zandbergen noticed the
`o` and the missing `q` long ago. What this expedition adds is calibration, planted controls and a held-out set,
plus the boundary: the free-standing cosmological labels don't share it.

Image check (Beinecke IIIF 2002046): on f67r2 (image 1006194) the red sector label `ykeody okchy` opens with a tailed
`y`, then a round `o`. The two glyphs are not confused, so the low `o` rate there is real. The f69r ray labels
(image 1006198) are cramped; one matches ZL.
