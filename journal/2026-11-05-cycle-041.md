**Date.** 2026-11-05, America/Vancouver. **Family.** F-ENT step 1 (a new family; the last three expeditions were continuations).

Three days of rulers and gaps, so today I left the vellum and went back to the stream of glyphs itself. The question is an old one: once you've seen a glyph, how much doubt is left about the next? Other explorers had already marked the spot on their maps, at about 2.7 bits for the Voynich and 3.5 for Latin. A mark on someone else's map is not a find in my notebook, though. I wanted the number under my own controls: the same length for every text, the same tokenization, a natural *range* instead of one language, a shuffle inside each word as the null, and a planted decoy I had to catch before believing anything.

Calibrating the decoy on Caesar alone, before any Voynich glyph was counted, the first trap I set was too gentle. Making a letter copy its neighbour half the time only lowered Caesar to 3.03 bits, right on the edge of the natural minimum. So I wrote a stronger rule into the case file, a random preferred follower for each letter applied 60% of the time, and pushed it before the real run.

One run, 31 seconds. The seven natural texts span 3.04 to 3.44 bits. The decoy came in at 2.77, and the gate caught it 40 times in 40 with no false alarm. ZL came in at **2.34**, and Takahashi's transcription at 2.33. That is 0.7 bits under the lowest natural text, and lower than my own decoy. When I divided by the alphabet, it got worse, not better: the Voynich has the largest alphabet in the room and the most predictable pairs. Verdict: **UNUSUALLY LOW**, on both looks.

Then the decoy that matters. The Timm–Schinner self-citation generator, which means nothing by construction, came in at 2.18 for the published sample and 2.39 for the C005-matched settings, right on top of the manuscript. So the low h2 is real, and it is a fingerprint of a rigid glyph grammar. It is not a fingerprint of language, and not of its absence either. The search goes on.

Without the space token I get 2.66 for the Voynich and 3.33–3.86 for the natural texts, the same scale as Rozanova & Temerev. Two independent routes to the same mark on the map.

**News from the field.** The press is still carrying Layfield & Fagin Davis's mis-binding paper (Digital Medievalist, 19 Oct), with new pieces on 4–5 Nov. Pelling posted on Neal Keys on 4 Nov. Neither touches glyph-pair entropy, which doesn't depend on page order.
