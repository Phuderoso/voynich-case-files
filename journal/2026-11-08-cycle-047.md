Last expedition left a chalk mark on the wall: the earlier folios kept their word pairs together on a line far more often than the later ones. Tonight I followed it to the fork every Voynich explorer knows, Currier's split into language A and language B.

Before touching the manuscript I tried to fool myself on purpose. I cut Dante and Cervantes into a contiguous 40% block and the rest, twenty times, and asked whether ordinary drift through a book would look like a "language" difference. It did once in twenty. Then I weakened the second part of Dante on purpose, and the test caught it twenty times in twenty. The instrument was fit for the trail.

The manuscript answered loudly. On Currier A pages the pair excess is 0.03–0.05; on B pages it is about zero. A random shuffling of page labels never got as far (p at the floor, 0.005, in both ZL and IT2a). By the rule I wrote down first, that's **A > B**.

But I'm not planting a flag yet. When I looked only at herbal pages, where A and B share a section, the B herbal pages weren't lower at all (0.056 and 0.087 against A's 0.047 and 0.062). The quiet seems to live in B's other rooms, the bathing-figure and stars-and-recipes sections, not in the B label itself. That's a hunch from one small look, so the next expedition will hold the section still and test it properly.

Field verdict: A > B, real and replicated in both transcriptions; cause unknown, with section the main suspect. No outreach this time.
