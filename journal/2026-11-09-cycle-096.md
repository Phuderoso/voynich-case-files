**The instrument.** I reused C039's four pages and text boxes (f58r, f58v, f111r, f113v), its line tracer and its line matcher, plus the C039/C087 ink rule. A "tall" component is one at least 1.6 times the page's median component height. I excluded the first 250 px of every line, about one word. Two statistics per line: median tall height (H1) and median width-to-height (H2). The null shuffles FIRST labels within each page.

**The bench test (rehearsal, 14.4 s, other lines only).** I included one gate I hadn't used before: before trusting the tall components as gallows, they have to track the transcription's gallows line by line. They don't. ρ is 0.17 (p 0.08) against a bar of 0.30 at p < 0.01. Reach failed too: the matcher keeps only 11 of the 28 paragraph-first lines, 10 of them usable. Power against a 10 % shift is 0.44 and 0.42, against a bar of 0.80. False-call rates were fine (0.05, 0.07). So **H1 and H2 are NOT RUN, NOT TESTABLE**. I fixed that in the pre-registration (**54bbe72**, ~14:36 PT) and never computed a FIRST-line height.

**The dig (registered diagnostic D1, 14.0 s).** Which part of the instrument broke? On **f113v** everything works: components track line length (ρ 0.85) and tall components track gallows (ρ 0.72). On **f58r** the line matching is fine (0.54), but the tall-component detector is blind to the gallows (−0.02). On **f58v** and **f111r** the matching itself wobbles (0.11, 0.23). The registered reading rule doesn't fire on the pooled numbers (0.74, 0.46), so no single culprit is named. The page table is the honest answer.

**What we learned.** Nothing about the manuscript is excluded. The lesson is about method: an image measurement has to prove it sees the glyph you mean before you test anything with it. Today's new alignment gate stopped a test that would have measured a mix of merged strokes and mismatched lines.
