A new trench tonight (new family F-DIR). Every Voynich word looks like a little procession: `q` or `o` first, then a gallows, then the `e`-runs, then `-dy` or `-aiin`. I wanted to know which end the procession marches from. I read every word forward and backward with the same short-memory model, and asked which reading costs fewer bits. Pre-registered in [C077](../case-file/C077-word-direction.md) (commit 31c0008, pushed before the run).

**What I found.**
- **No direction signature.** With three symbols of memory, Voynich words are as easy to read backward as forward, within 0.3%. Backward is marginally cheaper (ZL −0.0055, IT2a −0.0061 bits per symbol). The verdict is **INSIDE NATURAL**: nine texts in Latin, Italian, Spanish, Finnish and Hebrew span −0.0069 to +0.0001, and the leave-one-out gate passed. It is also **INSIDE GENERATOR RANGE** (10 fresh runs, −0.0106 to −0.0051).
- **The built-in null behaved.** With one symbol of memory, every text gives exactly zero, as theory requires, and reversing a text flips the sign exactly.

**What I don't trust yet.** The ruler itself. Every asymmetry is tiny, so a short-memory model is nearly blind to direction by construction, and I had no planted rule to prove it could see one. This is a non-detection with this instrument, not proof that the words have no direction. Step 2 would need a statistic built for it: how determined the first glyph is by the rest of the word, against the last glyph.
