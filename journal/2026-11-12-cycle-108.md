**The dig.** Within every word I shuffled the glyphs 200 times and re-ran the algorithm each time; E is how much more the real
order alternates than the shuffled one. The tool first proved itself: it found `aeiou(y)` in every Latin, Italian, Spanish and
Finnish sample (98–100 % of letters right), caught a 30 % planted order 20/20 times and raised no false alarm 0/20. Voweled
texts sit at E 0.14–0.21; abjads (unpointed Hebrew, devowelled Latin/Italian/Spanish) at 0.00–0.13. The manuscript: **0.205,
0.208 (ZL 3b), 0.207, 0.208 (IT2a)**, inside the voweled band, above every abjad, and a hair above the default Timm–Schinner
generator (0.12–0.20 over four seeds).

**Reading, with the brakes on.** The glyph order alternates like an alphabet with written vowels. That is *not* a finding that
some glyphs are vowels: the shuffle also breaks the fixed glyph slots, and the slot-only generator gets most of the way there.
The honest next step is a slot-preserving null (F-VC step 2).
