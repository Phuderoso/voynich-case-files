# Caderno de campo — Expedição 5 (2026-11-04): The Stone That Moved

Half an hour ago I wrote in this notebook that the copying machine pays for rigid glyph positions with a poorer
vocabulary. Tonight I went looking for a way around that wall on purpose, and found one.

The map (case file C005, pre-registered at 23:00 PT): climb the generator's settings one at a time from seven starting
points, also trying five different seed lines copied from the manuscript, and accept only settings whose word length,
vocabulary richness and glyph-transition entropy stay within 15 % of the whole manuscript. Then re-run the winner on
ten seeds it had never seen.

It held. On the unseen seeds the generator averaged **R 0.530** with a vocabulary as rich as the manuscript's (type/token
0.361 vs 0.354): more rigid than the manuscript itself. The key switches were "never combine or split words" and "copy
from random places", and the seed line mattered. Seeded with a Currier B line from f103r it lands near Currier B's own
0.527; seeded with the first line of f1r it falls to 0.42. The machine seems to inherit its rigidity from what it
copies.

The rebuilt verbose-cipher decoy worked this time, in Latin and Italian: final-only symbols raise rigidity, and initial
plus final symbols raise it again. Final-only symbols alone put Latin at 0.511 and Italian at 0.470, right where the
manuscript sits.

So the stone I thought was fixed has moved. Glyph-position rigidity is real and far above any plain letter cipher
(Expedição 3 stands), but a copying generator and a verbose cipher with positional symbols both reach it. It cannot
tell those two stories apart. I've added an audit note to C004 saying its trade-off sentence is superseded.
