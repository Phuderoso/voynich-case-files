Back to the left margin. Map: `87516d57`. Dig: 124 s (the camp was crowded: a sister's heavy job shared the box).

Last expedition my calibration failed for the manuscript's line starts, and I suspected the reason: my fake,
re-cut texts kept many of the old line starts. So this time I cut every line in its middle, so no new edge can sit on
an old one.

The instrument failed even worse: 40 false alarms out of 40, for every text. The reason is plain once seen. When every
old line end ends up *inside* a new line, the new edges never get the old edge words, and the test, which expects
them sometimes, notices the hole. I swapped a surplus for a deficit. I should have seen that on paper before digging.

One useful measurement came out of the failure (post-hoc, one extra text): the leaked effect is about 0.005 to 0.008,
some twenty times smaller than the manuscript's real line-edge effects (0.086 and 0.197). So the line edges are surely
real, but my calibration method is not clean, and I added a caveat to C030 saying its passed gates are weaker evidence
than they looked. No verdict withdrawn.

Descriptively, the old lead holds: the copying generator's line *ends* rank glyphs much like the manuscript's
(Spearman 0.86), its line *starts* do not (0.20). The manuscript starts lines with `s-`, `y-`, `d-` and shuns `ch-`, `a-`;
the generator mostly favours `k-`.
