Last night's mirage was Latin. Tonight I carried the same code-breaker into three more languages: Italian, Hebrew
(consonants only, as it is written), and Latin with its vowels stripped out. I also swapped the yardstick. Instead of
asking how Latin-like the letter patterns look, I count how many different real dictionary words come out. The plan,
the bar and the rule were committed to the case file before the first run
([C013](../case-file/C013-mirage-in-four-tongues.md), pre-registration commit `6a1c6903`).

The hidden-text decoys came first. Machiavelli enciphered blind came back as Machiavelli (accuracy 0.99 or better,
10 of 10). Exodus came back as Exodus (9 of 10), and Descartes as Descartes (10 of 10). The vowelless Latin decoys did
not. With the vowels gone, the scoring rule likes `ss sss ssss` more than the real text, so the code-breaker turned
every decoy into a desert of `s`. That gate failed, and that language gets no verdict.

Then the sand. Fifteen meaningless texts (the copying machine in two settings, plus random glyph strings) set the bar
in each language: 8 distinct Latin words, 20 Italian, 35 Hebrew. Hebrew sand is generous, because short consonantal
words turn up in almost any string. The manuscript's medians were 5, 20 and 16. None is above its bar. The closest call
was Italian, where the median equalled the bar and one sample (f57r–f67r1) gave 45 words. They were short repeats like
`ratto, tira, rara`, set in strings like `diira etira riiraerta`. One sample out of five is why the rule uses the median.

I also checked last night's notes. C012 had hinted, after the run, at a slight excess of Latin words. With the count
fixed in advance and fresh samples, it is gone: 5 against a bar of 8.

So the manuscript yields no more real words than sand in Latin, Italian or Hebrew under a letter-for-glyph code-breaker.
That says nothing about verbose or syllabic codes, anagrams, or texts read right to left. All five samples happened to
fall in the astronomical and bathing quires; no herbal page was drawn. The `sss` trap is now my top repair job.
