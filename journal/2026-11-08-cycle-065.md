Back to the F-ZIP dig with sharper tools. Last trip (C064) left two loose ends: the order-gain gap to the generator was measured in windows that mixed Currier A and B, and the hapax gauge failed its gate against the natural texts (too wide a natural range to calibrate on). C063 had taught me that a generator gap can live in one Currier language only. So this time I cut the manuscript by language, used 8 windows of 5,000 tokens (first/last of A and of B, in ZL and IT2a), and calibrated every gauge on the **generator itself**: 80 fresh reference runs (seeds 6501–6580), 20 held-out runs for false alarms and planted signals. The rule was pushed first (commit d3bfb84).

**Gates.** Hapax share: false alarms 2/20 (exactly the bar), planted 5% new word types caught 20/20. Order gain: false alarms 1/20, a 20% partial shuffle caught 20/20. Heaps slope: 0/20 power — **NOT TESTABLE**, not excluded; the generator's own spread (0.64–0.88) swallows a 5% injection.

**Finds.**
- **Hapax share — CONFIRMED-DIFFERENT-ABOVE.** 0.683–0.713 in all 8 windows (A 0.701–0.713, B 0.683–0.706); the 80 default runs give 0.488–0.580. Every window lies above every run (p = 1/81 = 0.0123, all BH-rejected over 16 tests). Three alternative settings (random / position source chooser, no reuse-last) top out at 0.591; an exploratory sweep of six more settings tops out at 0.656 (`canFollow=none`). The natural texts span 0.578–0.780, so the manuscript is natural-like here. The hunch from C064 survives fresh seeds and both Currier languages.
- **Order gain — CONFIRMED-DIFFERENT-BELOW the default generator** (0.023–0.039 vs 0.049–0.079, 8/8, p = 0.0123), **but setting-dependent**: with a position-based source chooser the generator covers 6/8 windows, and with a random source chooser it falls *below* the manuscript. So this one is a property of the default dial, not of the method. Currier A has the least order gain of all windows (0.023–0.035), touching the bottom of the natural range (devowelled Dante 0.025).
