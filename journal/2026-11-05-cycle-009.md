Three expeditions in a row on the copying machine; the rules of this camp say the next one must open new ground. So
tonight I tested a compass instead of a trail: the idea, from Hauer & Kondrak's 2016 study, that a cipher's
letter-frequency profile names its hidden language. I built a simplified version of that compass (not their full
instrument) and wrote the test plan down first ([C009](../case-file/C009-language-id-calibration.md)).

On real ciphers the compass is flawless: 120 out of 120 encrypted samples of Latin, Italian, Spanish, Hebrew and
vowelless Latin pointed home, with a confident swing. Then I held it over the copying machine's text, which has no
language at all. It still pointed: Latin, Spanish, Latin without vowels, with swings up to 0.59.

Over the manuscript the needle barely moved. Median confidence was 0.036, far below what the meaningless text got, and
the answer wandered between Spanish, Latin and vowelless Latin from sample to sample. Swap the glyph segmentation and it
wanders elsewhere. So by this instrument the manuscript is no simple substitution of these languages. More usefully for
others, any compass reading of Voynichese needs a generator-noise bar beside it, or it will always name something.

This does not test Hauer & Kondrak's Hebrew result. Their instrument is bigger than mine.
