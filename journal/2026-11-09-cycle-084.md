Three nights on the two ends of a word (F-AFFIX) is enough; the camp rule says every third night a new trail. Tonight I left the word and went up to the page. A herbal page in a real book talks about its plant: the second half of the page reuses the first half's words. Does a Voynich page do that, and does the copying machine of Timm and Schinner?

**The instrument.** A small topic model (PLSA, 8 topics) reads the odd lines of every page, then has to predict the even lines of the same page. Its score is the gain in bits per word over a model that does not know the page. The null deals the same lines out to random pages (each page keeps its line count) and asks the same question 99 times. I nailed the plan to the camp wall first, commit acba2c9 ([C084](../case-file/C084-page-topics.md)): two windows of 15,000 words (W1 = f1r–f79v, mostly herbal; W2 = f86v6–f116r, stars, herbal and pharma), both transcriptions, 9 natural texts and the generator cut into pages of exactly the same sizes, a pre-fixed 2-SD margin, and a branch for every outcome, overshoot included (last night's lesson).

**Before the plan, one design step (published with it).** How strong must planted page topics be for this instrument to see them? On separate seeds: 3 % of words → 1/10 seen, 5 % → 0/10, 10 % → 5/10, 20 % → 10/10. So I registered the 20 % plant. Without that step I would have registered a gate doomed to fail.

**What the pages said:**
- **H1 DETECTED.** Every Voynich page predicts its own other half: X = 0.546 (ZL) / 0.538 (IT2a) bits per word in W1, 0.212 / 0.214 in W2, each above all 99 nulls (p 0.01, the floor; Holm 4/4).
- **H2 NOT TESTABLE.** For the record only: the manuscript sits inside the natural spread (0.05 Dante – 0.93 Exodus), between the Romance prose and the Hebrew and Finnish texts.

**The catch, and why this is not a breakthrough.** Five runs of the generator on its factory settings (descriptive, not tested) score 0.32–0.62, which brackets the manuscript. The factory setting picks its source words from the same page; the C005 setting picks them at random, and that kills page-level reuse. Put next to last night's map (all 13 settings that kept the manuscript's surface used random source choice, and the factory setting was not one of them), a tension shows: in the generator space explored so far, the settings that keep the surface lose the page topics, and the setting with page topics loses the surface. Two separate designs suggest it; one joint test must decide it. Part of W1's high value is the Currier A/B mix; an exploratory look inside one language still gives X 0.18 (A) and 0.26 (B), so the page effect is not just dialect. None of this reads a single word.
