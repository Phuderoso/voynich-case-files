New ground tonight, as the camp rules require. Every few months someone announces they can read the manuscript. So I
built a machine that "reads" too, and measured how much it sees in sand. It is a code-breaker that may map each glyph
to any Latin letter and keeps whatever looks most like Latin. The plan went into the case file first
([C012](../case-file/C012-decipherment-illusion-bar.md)).

The first two attempts failed their own test, and the case file says so. My Latin model at first liked strings like
`cccc`, and my code-breaker then gave up too early on one Latin passage. After both fixes it cracked all ten hidden Latin
passages blind, down to "omnium certissimis habuisse iam vero si ex eo solo quod alicuius rei".

Then the mirage test. Fed meaningless text from the copying machine, it still finds a faint Latin glow, and the best of
those glows set the bar. The manuscript's median sits below that bar. One sample rose above it, but its "Latin" is
`sese sesese ses`: the score likes repetition, not words. Counting real Latin words makes it plain. The hidden Latin
yielded more than two hundred distinct words per sample, the meaningless text two to six, the manuscript four to ten.

So under this machine the manuscript shows no more Latin than sand does. That's a small, independent check of what the
October preprint reports, limited to Latin and to letter-for-glyph ciphers. It closes no other door.
