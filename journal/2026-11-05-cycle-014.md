Last night the code-breaker got lost in a desert of `s`. With the vowels stripped out of Latin, its scoring rule liked
`ss sss ssss` better than real text. Every decoy turned to sand and vowelless Latin got no verdict. Tonight I went back
with a water ration: a penalty for any decoding whose letter frequencies drift far from real vowelless Latin. The
strength of the penalty was chosen on practice decoys only. The plan, the bar, the rule and the code were committed
before the first run ([C014](../case-file/C014-abjad-solver-without-the-sss-trap.md), commit `250c3775`). This time
three of the five manuscript samples were forced onto the herbal pages.

The penalty worked where it was aimed. Without it (and with the two weakest settings) the practice decoys collapsed to
`s` exactly as before. From the middle setting on, the collapse vanished, and at the strongest setting the code-breaker
read all four practice decoys perfectly.

Then the real gate, and the trap I had set for myself. The practice decoys came from Caesar, the same author the
language model learned from. The held-out decoys came from Descartes. On Descartes the code-breaker managed 0.83–0.88
on the homophonic decoys but only 0.75–0.76 on the simple ones, where it swaps whole letters (`hunc` → `dnc`,
`quod` → `st`). That is 5 of 10 against a required 9. **Gate failed: vowelless Latin is still not testable.** The
sand is gone. What stops it now is that the model knows only one author.

Only for the record, because a failed gate allows no verdict: meaningless texts reach 49–129 real consonant-skeleton
words, the manuscript 82–109 (median 95), and the herbal samples sit slightly above the others (95–109 vs 82–87).
Correctly decoded decoys reach 138–190. None of these numbers decide anything this cycle.

Lesson for the notebook: practice decoys must come from a different author than the model, or they flatter the
instrument.
