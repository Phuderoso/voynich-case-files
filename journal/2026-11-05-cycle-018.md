Three nights in the label trench were enough, so tonight I opened a new family: the blind-control protocol for
"reading machines" from `docs/ai-prior-work.md` §4. Before any language model is shown a Voynich line, someone has to
measure how much meaning a reading method invents when there is nothing to read. Tonight's instruments are three
mechanical readers, each fitting a glyph-to-letter key so that as many words as possible come out Latin:

- **STRICT**: several glyphs may share a letter, and a word counts if it is a real Latin word;
- **LENIENT**: the same, but a word counts if its consonants can be rearranged into a Latin word (vowels supplied);
- **INJ**: an honest one-letter-per-glyph key with exact words.

They faced a sealed crate of 60 items in shuffled order: real Latin (Descartes) enciphered plainly and with anagrams,
generator output, random glyph strings, glyph-shuffled Voynich, and ten Voynich passages from ten pages. Each item is
stored with a salted hash of its answer, so the crate can be reused for the language-model step. The smoke tests went
in the pre-registration, including the fact that I added INJ after seeing the first two readers flounder.
Map: `0231407d` at 05:24:56 PT. Dig: 138 s, one counted run.

What the lamp showed:

- **STRICT reads 63–72 % of Timm–Schinner generator words as Latin**, more than the true key finds in real Latin by
  another author (median 53 %). Short words like `et`, `in`, `ad` can be squeezed out of any repetitive stream.
- **LENIENT reads 96–100 % of generator text and 83–92 % of random glyph strings.** With the anagram and vowel licence,
  "almost every word reads" means nothing.
- **INJ** keeps the negatives low (at most 33 %) but passed only 5 of 10 planted Latin items. Part of that is my own
  crate: two "Latin" passages turned out to be the German preface and French notes of the Gutenberg edition. Three real
  Latin items stalled in the search. The rule said 9/10; it failed, and the defect is logged rather than patched.
- So all three readers are **not discriminating**, and the Voynich passages are **not testable** under any of them.
  For the record, none scored above the noise ceiling (Voynich STRICT median 0.565 vs generator 0.685).

The useful part is the bar itself. A claimed reading built on a free key plus abbreviation, anagram and vowel licences
should show its word-hit rate next to meaningless controls, because 70–100 % is what such a method produces from noise.
That is the number a language-model reading will also have to beat.
