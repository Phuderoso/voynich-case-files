Same road as last time (F-TRX, step 2), but this time I sized the tools before the dig. Map: `a1bb14e2`. Dig: 46 s.

Last expedition left a sighting: the two copyists, Zandbergen and Takahashi, disagree most on the star pages. I
suspected the layout, not the scribe. So this time I compared lines **on the same page**: labels against paragraphs,
and ring and ray text against paragraphs. The page itself is the control, so scribe, language and drawing are fixed.

Both looks found it. Labels: 6.37 % disagreement against 1.92 % for paragraphs (within page +3.5 points, p 0.00025).
Rings and rays: 5.99 % against 3.10 % (+3.0 points, p 0.0022, only 10 pages). Shuffled controls gave 0 false alarms
in 40 tries, and a planted +1 point was found every time, so this time the test could see.

What I will not claim: which copyist is right, or whether the labels are harder because of the ink or because of
conventions. Only the scans can say. And this will not surprise old hands; what's new is a controlled number.
The use is practical: anything built on labels should be checked in both transcriptions first.

News (quick check): a Zenodo preprint (27 Oct 2026, [doi:10.5281/zenodo.23688172](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23688172))
tests the reordering step of Layfield & Fagin Davis on manuscripts of known structure. It finds that the page-similarity
signal is real where text runs across pages, but that the reordering gain does not by itself single out the proposed
orders. It does not measure the Voynich. It sits close to my C021–C023 bifolium cycles, which also found only a
conjugate-similarity excess and claimed no reading order.
