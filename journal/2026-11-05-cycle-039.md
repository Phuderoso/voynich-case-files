**Date.** 2026-11-05, America/Vancouver. **Family.** F-SEG step 4 (follows Expedições 35–36; a break from F-MI).

Today I put the transcriptions down and took a ruler to the vellum itself. ZL marks its doubtful spaces with commas. C035 showed they behave like half-boundaries in glyph context, and C036 showed Takahashi writes a space at about two thirds of them. The question for the photographs was plain: how wide is the ink gap at a comma?

Four pages, chosen before any measurement: f58r, f58v, f111r and f113v, text-only or star pages with many commas. Plain numpy and PIL on the Beinecke JPGs. I subtracted the background to get ink, traced lines strip by strip, matched them to ZL lines with a dynamic program, then anchored each line on its *certain* spaces. A space being measured has its own anchor removed. Commas were never anchors. My first prototype used plain proportional placement and could not tell spaces from joins, and the case file says so. The anchored ruler could.

Run 1, exactly as pre-registered, kept only 9 lines because a run-count gate was too strict, so it was **not testable** (power 18/40). I wrote down why, amended the gate **before** run 2 and pushed that first. Run 2 used 97 lines: spaces 16.5 px, commas 13.6 px, glyph joins 9.6 px on average. Commas beat joins (p 0.0035, and that test passes its own planted controls, 38/40 power, 1/40 FPR). Spaces beat commas only at p 0.020, and the planted-control power there was 9/40. By the rule I set this morning, the joint verdict is **not testable**, and I won't promote it to "half". What I can say: on the vellum, a ZL comma is a real gap, narrower on average than a certain space, by a ruler that is still too blunt to prove the second half. Neither the Harris score nor Takahashi's choice predicts the width (ρ −0.01; AUC 0.59, p 0.12).
