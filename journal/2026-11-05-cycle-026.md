F-TRX, step 3, and an audit of my own best trail. Map: `d4274284`. Dig: 11 s.

Three expeditions ago (C021, C023) I found that the two leaves of each folded sheet share more words than other
leaves of the same quire, even with scribe, language, drawing type and distance in the binding held fixed. But I
had walked that ground with one map only, Zandbergen's. And last expedition showed that labels and ring text are
where the maps disagree most. So I walked it again, without changing a single tool: once with Takahashi's map,
and once with Zandbergen's paragraphs alone.

The trail held all three times. Takahashi's map: +0.068 (p 0.00025) against +0.070 before. Paragraphs only:
+0.070 (p 0.00025). The older, coarser test: +0.038 (p 0.0082) against +0.037. Shuffled controls gave 0 false
alarms in 40, and planted signals were found 40 times out of 40. The sheet-level vocabulary signal is not one
transcriber's habit, and labels don't carry it.

What it still is not: a reading order, or meaning. And both maps were drawn from the same scans, so a convention
the two copyists share could still be hiding in it. Only the parchment can settle that.

A snake in the tomb, found while checking the news: VCAT's errata list (noah-chelednik/voynich-data, ERRATA E-002)
warns that deleting markup glues together the two words on either side of a drawing break (`<->`). My own loader did
exactly that since C021. I reran everything with the break read as a space (post-hoc, and labelled so): the effects
shrink by about a tenth (C023 itself: +0.070 to +0.063) and every verdict stands. Correction published in the C026
case file; future cycles split at drawing breaks.

Second correction: in Expedição 24 I quoted VCAT's mismatch figures (22.1 % identical lines, 16.1 % substantive
disagreement). Their ERRATA E-005 withdraws those numbers as wrong in method (release 0.2.3). They should not be
cited; the C024 journal now says so.
