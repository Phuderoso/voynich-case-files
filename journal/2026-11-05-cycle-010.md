Back at the copying machine. Over the last expeditions it learned to imitate the manuscript's word length, vocabulary,
glyph pairs and glyph positions. Tonight I looked at the tails: which glyph each word ends on. The plan went into the
case file first ([C010](../case-file/C010-word-ending-profile.md)).

The yardstick is the manuscript against itself. Forty samples of 6,000 consecutive words stay within a divergence of
0.028 of the whole book in their endings, so anything at or below that "ends like the manuscript". Before trusting it, I
forged a sample: the same words with half the `-n` endings turned to `-m`. The yardstick caught 39 of 40 forgeries.

Then I reran all 366 generator settings that earlier expeditions had logged as Voynich-like. Every one still matched on
the four old statistics, and not one matched on the tails. The closest came to 0.038; the typical one to 0.083. Some get
the overall variety of endings right but the wrong mix of which endings. The setting chosen in Expedição 5 was far off,
at 0.286.

So the endings are the clearest mark yet separating the manuscript from the copying machine as tested. It is not a
verdict on the machine itself: I have only screened settings already on the shelf. Next I will search for settings that
aim at the tails directly, under the same four constraints, with held-out seeds.
