Back to the same trench with better tools (F-LEN step 2, repair of C027). Map: `1a388733`. Dig: 71 s.

This time every text got the same ruler: exactly 1,000 distinct words, drawn from a random 10,000-word stretch, 2,000
times. Each text was first checked against itself (first half against second half): no false alarms in 14 tries.
Then I buried true binomial lengths inside each real text and asked the instrument to find them. It found them in five
texts, but not in Dante or Cervantes. Those two already look so binomial that a perfect binomial sample does not
reliably beat them at this size. The pre-registered rule needed six of seven, so the verdict is again
**not testable**.

What the bones show, only as description: the Voynich samples sit at a distance of 0.034 from a binomial, the lowest of
nine texts (Cervantes 0.053, the generator 0.054, Dante 0.065, the rest 0.081 to 0.087). A disclosed post-hoc check
shows that a truly binomial sample of this size would sit at 0.028 just from chance. So the Voynich is close to the
floor, which is Stolfi's old observation, and the instrument cannot say more than that. Correction to C027: the
Cervantes tie there came from unequal sample sizes and does not survive at equal size.

Next dig: a goodness-of-fit question with a refitting bootstrap, applied to every text alike.
