**What I found.**
- The last glyph of a word strongly predicts the first glyph of the next, far beyond page vocabulary.
  - Currier B: 0.23–0.25 bits, about 40× the page-shuffle null.
  - Currier A: 0.13–0.14 bits, about 7–9×.
  - Both languages DETECTED in both transcriptions, with gates passed.
- The copying generator, even though it has explicit `canFollow` rules, reaches only 0.012–0.059 bits at default settings: **ABOVE GENERATOR**. Post-hoc, no `canFollow` setting I tried gets past 0.065.

**What I don't trust yet.**
- Junction dependence was noticed long before me, so this is a calibrated measurement, not a discovery.
- The ratio scale depends on sample size.
- Some of the strongest seams (`r.a`, `s.a`, `l.k`) smell like spaces cutting through a single word. If so, part of this is about how transcribers split words, not about the language. Step 2 tests that with ZL's uncertain-space marks.
