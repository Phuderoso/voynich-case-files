Third survey of the margins. Map: `89cde510`. Dig: 47 s.

Two failed instruments taught me the rule: a text that has real lines cannot be cut up to make a fair "no lines" text,
because the old lines always leave tracks. So this time the fair test texts have every word shuffled across the whole
book (no lines left at all), and the tracks are measured separately: the effect has to be at least three times the
biggest track any re-cut text left.

Both margins hold. Line ends and line starts are real in the manuscript, in both transcriptions, and in the copying
generator too; every instrument check passed. The manuscript's line start is the closest call (4.9 times its worst
track, against a bar of 3), so I note it, but it passes.

Then the question I really came for. The manuscript starts lines with `s-`, `y-` and `d-` words more than twice as
often as chance within the line (1,575 vs 700). Does the generator? No: 212 against 242 expected, slightly *fewer*
than chance. The instrument could have seen it (40 of 40 planted cases caught). So in the authors' published sample,
the copying machine learns where lines end, but not how they begin.

One sample is one sample. Before I knock on the generator authors' door, Expedition 33 will rerun this test on several
generator settings with the jar on hand.
