New territory tonight. After three expeditions with code-breakers, I left the solvers in camp and went to read the little
name tags scattered over the drawings: the words beside the bathing nymphs and their pipes, around the Rosettes
foldout, on the zodiac rings and stars, and next to the jars and root fragments of the pharmacy pages. If those
tags name things, the tags of a bath and the tags of a jar might use different words or different glyphs.

The trap was obvious before I set out: each kind of drawing was written by a different scribe in a different
dialect, so the tags would differ for that reason alone. So the map
([C015](../case-file/C015-label-vocabulary.md), commit `a776a40b`) asked a relative question: do the tags differ
more, or less, than ordinary words taken from the running text of the very same pages? A planted shift had to be
caught 9 times in 10 before any answer counted.

It wasn't caught at all: 0 of 10, in every pair and both measures. The decoy I planted (swapping `k`/`t` and
`ch`/`sh` in half the tags) turned out to be a quiet one, because those glyphs live in very similar places. And
there are too few tags: 119 for the baths, 152 on the Rosettes, 243 for plants and jars. Afterwards, and labelled as
exploration, I tried louder decoys. Even sticking a `q` in front of half of a group's tags, a glyph tags almost never
start with, was caught at most 8 times in 10. **Verdict: not testable.** The instrument is too small for this
territory.

One thing caught the lamp on the way out, and I'm marking it on the map as a lead, not a find. In all three pairs
the tags look *more like each other* than like their own pages' text (Rosettes p 0.001, zodiac 0.007, baths 0.107).
Most of that is the letter they start with: 51–66 % of tags begin with `o`, against 17–39 % of the running text on
bath, zodiac and plant pages. `otedy` is the most common tag both in the baths and on the Rosettes. Tags seem to be
one genre across the whole book. That direction had no planted control tonight, so it stays a lead.

Image check: I looked at f77r and f99r in the Beinecke scans (IIIF manifest 2002046). The tags sit where ZL says
they do (on the pipes and nymphs, on the jar, over each fragment), and four of the f77r tags read as transcribed.
Three are too cramped to confirm.
