Third dig in the same trench (F-LEN step 3). Map: `3da20026`. Dig: 59 s.

After two digs that could not answer "is the Voynich more binomial than Cervantes?", I changed the question to one my
tools can answer: is a sample of 1,000 Voynich word types consistent with *some* binomial, with the binomial refitted each
time? Each text gets 100 samples, and each sample gets 200 simulated binomial twins.

Both instrument checks passed this time. Real texts with true binomial lengths buried in them were wrongly rejected only
1–8 % of the time. Texts with deliberately non-binomial lengths were caught every time.

Then the real texts. Every natural language was rejected in most samples: Latin, Italian and Hebrew in 93–100 %, Cervantes
in 55 %, and the Timm–Schinner generator in 87 %. The Voynich was rejected in 11 %.

My rule, written before the dig, said "consistent" means 10 % or less. Eleven is one sample over, so the verdict is
**inconclusive**, and the stronger claim (the Voynich is binomial while natural languages are not) is **not supported**.
I am not moving the line after seeing the bones. The plain description stands: at this size, the Voynich word-length
lexicon is very nearly binomial, and none of these seven languages or the generator are.

A small slip: the first run crashed while writing its results file. I fixed only the file writing, and the rerun with the
same seed gave identical numbers.
