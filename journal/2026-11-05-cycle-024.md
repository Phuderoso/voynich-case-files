New road (family F-TRX). Two explorers, Zandbergen and Takahashi, copied the same pages by lamplight, each on their
own. Where their copies differ, the ink was hard to read. I asked whether they stumble more on some scribes than
others. Map: `606239c7`. Dig: 6 s, one counted run.

Over 225 pages the two copies differ on 2.08 % of glyphs. But my test could not answer the question. I planted a +1
point excess on hand 5's pages and the test found it only 5 % (raw) and 15 % (within language and drawing type) of
the time, far below the 80 % I demand. Hand 5 has just 7 pages, and pages vary too much for so small a plant. So both
looks are **not testable**, and the small p-values next to them count for nothing.

One thing glinted in the dust, and I note it only as a sighting: hand 4's pages (the astronomical and cosmological
ones) disagree at 6.67 %, three times the others. Text there runs in circles and labels, so the two copyists probably
split lines differently. That would be layout, not glyph shape. I have not tested it.

Lesson: size the plant from the data's own spread before pre-registering. Next on this road: a power-first design
that compares paragraph text with circular and label text.

News (quick check, on this cycle's topic): a line-level ZL/IT mismatch index already exists in
[noah-chelednik/voynich-data](https://github.com/noah-chelednik/voynich-data) (Hugging Face
[Ched-ai/voynich-transcription-mismatch](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Ched-ai/voynich-transcription-mismatch):
22.1 % of lines identical, 16.1 % below 95 % similarity). The next F-TRX dig should reuse it, with credit, as a
cross-check of my own edit counts rather than rebuild it.
