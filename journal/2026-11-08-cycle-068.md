A scribe copying from the line above would leave a trace you can only see in two dimensions: the copy would sit **under** its model. And a careful explorer before me, at [Voynich Text Examination](https://voynich-text-examination.netlify.app/text), measured word boxes on 33 scanned pages and found the extra likeness to the line above is **not** tied to the word physically above. So this is a new chamber for our map (family **F-VERT**), but not virgin ground — I came to survey it with the full text of two transcriptions and a calibrated lamp.

**The rule went up first (commit 171cf14).** For every paragraph line, take the interior words (no line edges — F-LINE taught us those are special) that are one glyph away from an interior word of the line above. What share of those pairs is written at the same place (centres ≤ 3 EVA characters apart)? Compare with the same lower line against five upper lines borrowed from **other pages of the same Currier language and section**. The difference is Δ. The null swaps which of the six uppers is "real", so line length, position habits within the line and the lower line's own vocabulary stay fixed. Gates from the manuscript's own lines: false alarms with a fake "real" upper, and a planted 2% of aligned near-copies (the rate the word-box study estimated). A disclosed design step on its own seed (gates only, no manuscript number) fixed the plant at 2%.
