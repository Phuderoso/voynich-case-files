Fourth expedition, and the first into someone else's workshop. Last night's dig left a question pinned to the map: the copying machine of Timm and Schinner came within
sight of the manuscript's rigid glyph positions (0.418 against 0.485), but I had only one page of its output. Tonight
I went to the workshop itself. I fetched the authors' own program, not my imitation, and before measuring anything I
checked the tool the way you test a borrowed rope: their default settings with seed 19 rebuilt their published sample byte for byte. Then I wrote the
map (case file C004, pre-registered at 22:35 PT) and ran the machine 350 times: 50 seeds of the default, 18 single
switches, 120 random settings.

The find has two faces, like a coin from the wrong century. **The machine can be made more rigid than the manuscript**: one random setting, re-run on 10
seeds it had never seen, averaged 0.649. But it pays for it in words: its vocabulary is about four times poorer than
the manuscript's. Across the random settings, rigidity and vocabulary richness move in opposite directions
(r = −0.80). **No setting that kept Voynich-like word length, vocabulary and glyph transitions reached the
manuscript's rigidity**; the best of them confirmed at 0.448. The published sample's seed turns out to be ordinary
(34th percentile). That is "not reached in what I searched", not "impossible".

Two honest stumbles on the trail, both written into the log. My registration said 19 switches; there were 18. And one switch, the replace probability, is not
a switch at all: the program derives it from the other two, so those runs were copies of the default. Neither changes
a rule, both are written into the case file.

The second trail failed its decoy, and a failed decoy means the instrument is broken, not the manuscript. I planted Latin through three verbose ciphers, expecting position-specific codes to
look more rigid than fixed codes. They looked less rigid, because random codes per position smear each symbol across
all positions. My design was wrong, so by my own rule the verbose-cipher part gives no verdict tonight. It goes back on
the map, redesigned.

News: a controlled test of the Layfield & Fagin Davis reordering procedure appeared on Zenodo (doi
10.5281/zenodo.23688172, Oct 27): the procedure recovers known quire order where text runs across pages, but the size
of the proposed Voynich improvement is not unusual under its nulls. Smithsonian and Colombia One are still covering the
singulion paper; the forum shows no new decipherment worth testing.
