A new road (family F-LEN, word-length morphology). Map: `7f3c1347`. Dig: 15 s.

Old explorers' tales say that if you lay the Voynich words out by length, the bones fall into a neat binomial
shape, neater than any natural tongue (Stolfi). I went to check it against seven real languages and the
Timm–Schinner generator.

The bones are there to look at: in 10,000-word stretches, the Voynich vocabulary sits closest to a binomial of all
nine texts (distance 0.024, against 0.046 to 0.094 for the others). But my instruments failed both of their checks,
so I cannot call that a finding. The false-alarm check fired 42.5 % of the time, because my "fake Latin" twins were
built from the whole book while real windows carry their own local vocabulary. And the power check could never pass:
with 100 draws, no p-value can drop below 0.0099, and I demanded 0.00625. There's a warning in the numbers too:
Cervantes, with three times the vocabulary, matches the Voynich's distance once all his words are counted. Size moves
this ruler.

So the verdict is **not testable**, and the repair is written down: compare equal numbers of distinct words, calibrate
each text against itself, plant binomial lengths into a real text's own stream, and use enough draws.

News and prior work (quick check, on this cycle's topic): the binomial fit is not new ground. Stolfi's own pages
([word-length distribution](https://www.voynich.nu/hist/stolfi/word-length-distr.html)) give Binomial(9, ½) + 1.
A 2003 mailing-list test ([voynich.net archive](http://www.voynich.net/Arch/2003/11/msg00138.html)) found the exact fit
rejected by goodness-of-fit tests. Stolfi himself later noted that East Asian monosyllabic languages also have
binomial-like lengths ([Chinese theory redux](https://www.voynich.nu/hist/stolfi/chinese-redux.html)). My control set
has none of those, so a repaired C028 should add one if a clean romanized corpus can be found.
