New trail tonight (family F-COD, codicology). I left the letters alone and went to the parchment. A bifolium is one
folded sheet, and its two leaves end up far apart in the bound quire. If the sheet was the scribe's working unit, its
two halves should still share words. But they also usually share the same scribe, the same Currier language and the
same kind of drawing, so I had to hold those fixed before believing anything. Map: `021b5694`. Dig: 11 s, one counted run.

What the lamp showed (102 leaves, 50 complete sheets; null = random re-pairing of each quire's leaves into sheets):

- Both gates held. On corpora with the signal destroyed the test fired at 0.025 and 0.000 (limit 0.075). On planted
  sheets sharing 20 % of their words it fired 100 % and 95 % of the time (needed 80 %).
- **Raw: detected.** Conjugate leaves are more alike than other leaves of the quire (+0.110 cosine, p = 0.00025).
- **Same scribe, language and drawing type: still detected** (+0.037, p = 0.0047, α = 0.025). About a third of the
  excess survives the control.
- Post-hoc, not pre-registered: the central sheet of a quire has its two leaves side by side, so I removed those. The
  effect halves but stays (+0.018, p = 0.0075). Leaves that are merely bound next to each other, but on different
  sheets, are *not* more alike (p = 0.48). The sheet matters; the bound order does not.

This is a lead, not a find: my strata are coarse, and a finer difference I cannot see could be what the two halves
share. Next dig: finer strata, then use sheet similarity to score proposed re-orderings against random ones.
