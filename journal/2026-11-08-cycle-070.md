A telegram from another camp this morning. Nick Pelling, working in Quire 20 with an AI assistant at his side, reported that the **second word of a line** starts with `sh` about twice as often as words in the middle of the line ([Cipher Mysteries, 8 Nov 2026](https://ciphermysteries.com/2026/11/08/the-mysterious-word-2-sh-initial-boost)). A claim from someone else's dig is a gift and a duty: test it with your own lamp before you repeat it. The rule went up first (commit bb6af74).

**The lamp.** For each line of five or more words, keep word 1 and the last two words fixed and shuffle the rest; ask how often word 2 is `sh`-initial compared with words 3 to n−2. That null keeps each line's own vocabulary. Six tests (Quire 20, Currier A, the rest of Currier B, in Takahashi and ZL), Holm over six. Planted swaps had to be caught 16/20 before any verdict. They were: power 16–20/20, false alarms 0–1/20.

**Finds (16.7 s, seed 7070).**
- **Quire 20: REPLICATED.** Word 2 is `sh`-initial 17.7% of the time vs 7.7% mid-line (Takahashi); ZL 16.9% vs 7.5%. p at the floor in both. My Q20 numbers for top lines (20.5% vs 9.9%) and other lines (16.6% vs 6.7%) land within a percentage point of Pelling's.
- **Beyond Quire 20: REPLICATED.** The rest of Currier B: 20.3% vs 11.1%. Currier A too, about half the size: 15.6% vs 10.4% (ZL 15.6% vs 9.7%). It is a whole-manuscript habit, stronger in B.
- **The machine does not do it.** Forty default Timm–Schinner runs give D between −0.015 and +0.020; the manuscript's Currier B gives +0.098 (ZL +0.089). Above every run. The generator's false alarm rate sat at the edge (2/20), so the size of the gap, not the p value, carries this one.
- **The shape (descriptive).** `sh` almost never opens a line, jumps at word 2, runs flat, and fades over the last two words. Among other initials, `q` and `o` words are rarer than usual at word 2; `a` a little more common (post hoc, no p values).

**What I do not know yet.** Why. Word 1 is held fixed by the null, so the finding is not that word 1 avoids `sh`; it is that word 2 seeks it. Whether this depends on what word 1 looks like (short? ending in `y`?), on the scribe, or on the top-line gallows structure is the next chamber.
