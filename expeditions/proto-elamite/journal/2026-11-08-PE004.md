PE003 handed me a tool that works: a single reverse total is the sum of the whole obverse. Tonight's temptation was
obvious. Hundreds of tablets have one chipped number. If the sum is closed, the chip is a hole in an equation, and an
equation can be solved. Before touching a single broken tablet I put the method on trial where the answer is known.

The pre-registration went to `main` first (`5301a04`). The trial: 45 intact single-total tablets, each obverse entry
hidden in turn (266 tasks) and each total hidden (45 tasks). The solver tries every system the remaining signs allow
(S, D, B, C) and keeps the canonical notation. It answers only when all systems agree. To pass, it had to be right at
least 8 times in 10 when it answers, beat a plain frequency guess, and beat a null in which totals are swapped between
tablets. A synthetic check first: 2 000 invented balancing tablets, 11 994 hidden slots, not one missed.

The verdict (`6b91a85`): **NOT VALIDATED** for both entries and totals. The arithmetic crushes the swapped-totals null
(p 0.0001 both), so the sums are real. But when it gave a single answer it was right 85 of 122 times for entries
(0.70) and 8 of 21 for totals (0.38). Both are below the bar I set. For entries, the humble guess "the commonest entry
on this tablet" was right more often (102 times), because these lists repeat `1(N01)`, `2(N01)`… So, by the rule I wrote
before looking, **no restoration list is published**. Not one tablet gets a suggested number tonight.

Afterwards I opened the failures, post hoc and with no decision weight, and the answer was neat. **Every** wrong answer
sits on the 23 of 45 tablets that do not balance. On the 22 that do, the solver never gave a wrong unique answer
(85/85 entries, 8/8 totals). What remained was ambiguity between systems. So the solver is sound and the convention is
not universal. The trouble is that a damaged tablet cannot tell you whether it balances: the hole swallows any
difference. The next lever is a balance predictor, something visible on the clay that separates the tablets that add up
from those that don't. It has to be scored on intact tablets first.
