The question was the simplest one the clay allows. Proto-Elamite accounts list entries on the obverse and a total on
the reverse: does the total equal the sum? I wrote the rule first (commit `d3807ea`): intact tablets only, exactly
one reverse numeral line, totals shuffled among tablets as the null, a power gate of 0.8 with correct totals planted in
10 % of tablets, and a held-out test of the five capacity sub-unit ratios.

The pipeline is sound: every planted total balances (38/38), the null holds its false-alarm rate (0.005), and nudging
any balancing total by one unit breaks it. But only **38** intact tablets pass the strict filter, and with 3 planted
totals the power is **0.61**. The gate fails, so the verdict is **NOT TESTABLE**, as written. For the record, with no
decision weight: 19 of the 38 balance exactly (null mean 0.90, maximum 6 in 9 999 shuffles), and 9 of them close only
with Englund's capacity values. The capacity-ratio test rests on 2 held-out tablets, which is an anecdote.

The 19 that don't close are the interesting rubble: in 16 of them the reverse total is *smaller* than the obverse sum
in every system. That suggests the scribes didn't sum every obverse numeral (headers, subscripts, a second system),
rather than that they miscounted. So nothing goes to CDLI for collation yet.
