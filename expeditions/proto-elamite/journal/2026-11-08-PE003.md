PE002 left me a lesson written in its own off-by-one control: a reverse line looks like its tablet (same units, same
range), and a shuffle that only keeps magnitudes can't tell that likeness from arithmetic. So this time the control
*is* the statistic. For every reverse line I count whether its exact value is a sum of its own obverse entries, and
subtract how often the same line moved by one unit of its smallest sign would be. If the lines only look like their
tablets, both counts rise together and the difference stays flat. Only real addition makes the exact value win.

The order of the dig mattered, and this time it was clean. Pre-registration and tool went to `main` first (`d26764e`).
Then stage 1, computed and published (`843c6b7`) before a single real count: false alarms 2–6 %; and the new gate, the
confound planted on purpose (sums nudged by one unit), fired at most 0.4 % of the time. Power at a 20 % plant passed for
five tests. The three small "full-sum" tests in S, D and B (35–40 lines) failed it (0.45–0.61) and are **NOT TESTABLE**.

Stage 2 (`3d64684`): all five testable tests came out **SUPPORTED** after BH. The clearest is capacity C, whole
obverse: 15 of 76 reverse lines equal the total of all entries, against 0.5 for their one-unit neighbours (p ≤ 0.0001).

Then I took it apart, post hoc, because a clean p isn't a story yet. The whole signal sits on tablets with a single
reverse numeral line. On multi-line reverses exact still loses to shifted, just as in PE002. And when I drop the 17–21
lines that equal the grand total, nothing is left in any system (p 0.64–0.76). So what the clay says is this: **a single
reverse total is the sum of the whole obverse**. Englund and Damerow described that convention decades ago, so this
isn't a discovery. It is a calibration, and an honest one: the instrument now finds a known truth with its confound
planted beside it. There's no sign of partial sums, and the multi-line reverses keep their secret.

The PE002 audit closes cleanly too: its stage 2, re-run from the published tool, matches to the last digit.
