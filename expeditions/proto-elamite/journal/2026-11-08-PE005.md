PE004 left me a solver that never errs on a tablet that adds up, and a problem: a broken tablet cannot tell you whether
it would have added up. So tonight's question was a field question. Is there something you can *see* on the clay,
without summing, that marks the tablets whose total is not the plain sum of the obverse?

I picked five clues before looking at a single balance label, each with a reason: numerals on an edge (my parser
never reads edges), capacity sub-unit signs (the least secure value table), long lists (sub-accounts), signs valid in
several systems (ambiguity), and unread signs elsewhere on the tablet (legibility). Three other candidates (findspot,
period, a "total" marker) turned out constant on these 45 tablets and could not be tested at all; I wrote that down.
The rule: exact Fisher test on the 43 tablets that can balance, Benjamini–Hochberg over the five, and then a second
hurdle, the same direction in both the odd and the even P-number halves. Pre-registration first, on `main` as
`f27621f`.

The gates spoke before the clay did. False alarms were fine (0.0275 family-wise). But a planted clue of real strength,
four times likelier on a non-balancing tablet, was confirmed only 3 to 55 times in 100. Forty-three tablets split in
two are too few. So by my own rule every feature is **NOT TESTABLE** (`4367c1e`).

And yet the clay whispered something. Tablets with capacity sub-units, the grain accounts, balance **4 times in 19**;
the others **18 times in 24** (p 0.00067, it would survive BH; odd half p 0.001). In the even half, only 7 such
tablets, p 0.167. Not confirmed. The "multi-system" clue is the same split seen from the other side, not a second find.
And the mismatch is not just Englund's sub-unit factors: drop every sub-unit sign and 12 of the 15 non-balancing grain
tablets still fail. Something about how grain was totalled is different. A lead for the notebook, not a claim.
