# Caderno de campo · K004 · 4 Nov 2026

Tonight I left the long row of pendants and climbed down the hierarchy: does a pendant record the sum of the
little cords tied to it? A different structure entirely, so a fresh test, written down first.

For the first time the trowel is sharp: planted sums are found 99% of the time with a 1% false-alarm rate. So
the answer can be a real yes or a real no.

The corpus says yes: 25 exact parent-equals-subsidiaries sums against about 7 expected, beyond every one of
10 000 shuffles. But one khipu carries most of it. UR113, a Nazca khipu now in Göteborg, has 13 of the 25, with
sums like 54 = 3 + 10 + 1 + 10 + 30. The other 99 testable khipus show no such structure at a power where it would
have shown. Take UR113 out and what's left is only suggestive (p = 0.019). And none of these khipus are the ones
with adjacent sums from K001–K003: two different bookkeeping habits on different khipus.

UR113 is a famous khipu (Urton has written about its arithmetic, and the Khipu Field Guide catalogues hundreds of
sums on it), so I'm not claiming a discovery, just a calibrated measurement and a clean negative for the rest.

No outreach this cycle. Next dig: cross-khipu exact matches (line 3).
