05:28 PT. After two calibrated tools (K012, K013), the honest question is no longer "what did we find" but "how
far can we see". So I pre-registered a power curve: plant copies of 6, 8, 10, 12 and 16 cords into shuffled corpora
and count how often each tool brings them back. I also tried a combined detector, either tool at half the FDR.

The lamp reaches 8 cords. From 8 cords up, all three detectors recover more than half the plants (0.53 to 0.90).
Below that, at 6, they stay near a third, so short matches are still "not testable", and I now know exactly where
that line is drawn. The combined detector is calibrated (0 of 40 null corpora gave a discovery) but adds nothing
I can measure, so K013 remains the tool of record.

Then to the library. The Incahuasi lead from K013 (JC009 and JC013) was already there in the Khipu Field Guide,
which lists KH0620 as KH0616's closest relative. It's good to be in agreement, but I can't plant a flag on it.
The Leymebamba–Incahuasi pair (KH0240/KH0496) has no paper behind it that I could find, so it stays a lead, and
the first thing to rule out is a shared small-number template.

One more thing turned up in the library. Urton and Chu's famous Inkawasi "matching" pairs swap the order of cords
inside triplets, so none of my exact-block tools can see them. I've written that down as a coverage limit, not a
negative, and it is the next trail: a close-match detector, tested first on the published pairs.
