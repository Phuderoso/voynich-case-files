This morning K085 left the Inkawasi pair in a strange place: it passed both stages, but a positive control fell 1.4
points under a bar that the strongest true pairs had lifted. Tonight's question was only about the instrument. I did
not invent a new statistic; I took the old remedy for exactly this disease, the **step-down max-T** of Westfall & Young
and Romano & Wolf: once a pair is rejected, it leaves the null maximum, and the bar is recomputed over what is left.
It can only add discoveries to K020's single step, and its family-wise error stays at 0.05 because each pair's null is
the shuffle of its own two khipus. Pre-registration, tool and the OKR rebuild went to main first (`fb21b56`).

The run took 16 seconds. Calibrated (2/20), all three controls in both stages, and **KH0502·KH0514 CONFIRMED** on the
KFG (W 126.5, 12 groups, V 13, p₂ < 10⁻⁴). KH0491·KH0503 stays stage 1 only. Nothing new.

Then the honest part, the one I want in ink: the step-down was not what saved the control. With this seed the
single-step bar was already 76.2. Four seeds of the same 100-corpus null have now given bars of 74.8, 82.6, 80.3 and
76.2. A control 1.4 points under one of those bars is inside the coin toss of the null sampler. The leak is real (the
floor without it is 73.7), but its effect on a single run is a lottery over how many null corpora draw a leaked high.
The step-down's final bar never contains a rejected pair, so it is the rule to keep; and the next design for this
family should use 1 000 null corpora and print a bootstrap interval for every bar.
