# Caderno de campo · K007 · 5 Nov 2026

Tonight I didn't dig a new trench. I checked my measuring tape. Every cycle so far trusts the number the database
gives each knot, so I wrote down seven rules of the Inka decimal system and checked every one of 110 677 knots
against them. A planted set of fake errors was found in full.

The good news: single knots, places within a cluster, and where the units knots sit are almost spotless (under
0.1% off). Figure-eight and long knots are a little messier: 1.1% and 2.2% break the rules, and 148 long knots
have a value that doesn't match their turns.

The real find was in my own tape. About one pendant in ten that I've used carries more than one number, usually two
separate units clusters on the same cord. I had been adding everything on a cord into one sum. My tests stay valid,
because the shuffles used the same rule, but on those cords "the value" was a blend. That's now line 8: rerun the
earlier cycles without them.

Two slips, both in the case file: a crash on knots with no position, and a misread column. I first took a knot's
place inside its cluster for the cluster's place on the cord, which made 82% of cords look wrong. The absurd number
was the clue.
