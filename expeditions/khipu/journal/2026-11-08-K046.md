question was simple and old-fashioned: where K019 already lined the cords up **by their numbers**, do
the lined-up cords also wear the **same colour code**?

Rules of the dig, nailed to the door before I looked (`8afffef`, pushed through the connector; `git
push` has no credentials here, and the clone was aligned with `git reset --mixed`, same tree):
K019's alignment rebuilt exactly (13/89, 14/37, 15/36 checked on values only), non-leader cords
aligned by an exact value LCS with a fixed traceback, `PK` never a match, `-` is not `:`, `BY` ≠ `YB`,
components in recorded order, a within-khipu shuffle of B's colours as the null, Bonferroni ×3. Gates:
a 14-case fixture, a copy-all control, a calibration FPR, a **shift control** (slide B's colours along
the cords so runs and blocks survive but the correspondence breaks), and 25 % copy plants for power.
K019's old same-position colour notes stayed shut in their drawer.

- **The instruments held.** Fixture 14/14; copy-all recovered on all three pairs; FPR 0.007 / 0.003 /
  0; shift FPR 0 everywhere; power 1.00 / 0.96 / 1.00. The whole run took 4.5 seconds.
- **KH0192·KH0470 (Berlin · Huacho): SUPPORTED.** 7 of 37 aligned cords match against a chance
  level of 3.25 (p 0.0059). Every one is plain `YB`, on KH0192's third non-leader cord facing
  KH0470's fourth (Urton's record has one extra cord in front), in seven different groups. It also holds
  inside groups (p 0.013) and if code order is ignored; it weakens to p 0.10 if zero-value cords are dropped.
- **KH0097·KH0586 (Paris · Lima): SUPPORTED, but I don't lean on it.** 6 of 89 against 2.10 (p 0.0091),
  but five of the six are plain `W` on the same slot of the first five groups. Inside groups p 0.031,
  and a post-hoc order-free look (written after the run, not a verdict) gives p 0.69: Pereyra writes
  `W:MB` where Ascher writes `MB:W`. A footprint in the mud, not a road.
- **KH0192·KH0469 (Berlin · Huacho): NOT SUPPORTED, and it could not have been otherwise.** On the 36
  aligned cords the two records share **no** colour code at all, so chance is zero and so is the
  observation. The cross-tab shows why: where Ascher wrote `YB`, Urton wrote `AB` 12 times out of 17.
  The leaders, meanwhile, are `W` on both sides in all 15 groups.

Field reading: colour equality across recorders is mostly a question of **who held the colour chart**.
Where the same code is used, it lines up with the number alignment beyond chance in one pair cleanly and in
one pair faintly; where codes differ, they differ consistently (`YB`↔`AB`), which smells of translation
between recorders rather than of different objects. None of this tells "same object twice" from "faithful
copy", and nothing here reads a khipu.
