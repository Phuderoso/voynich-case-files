# Caderno de campo · K011 · 5 Nov 2026

Tonight I tried a finer net for short matches: give every pair of khipus its own chance probability, then let
Benjamini–Hochberg sort the real catches from the noise. I wrote the rules down first and committed them before
the net touched water.

The net failed its first test, and that test is the reason I run them. I cast it into 40 seas I had stirred
myself, where each khipu keeps its own runs but nothing links one khipu to another. It came back with false fish
every single time, 68 on average. On the real sea it brought up 72. That's no catch at all.

The culprit is runs. A khipu with long rows of 2s or 5s looks, to my formula, like a once-in-a-million
coincidence when it meets another khipu with the same habit. The formula treats every cord as a fresh coin toss,
and runs break that. So this tool is **excluded** here, and short matches stay **not testable**. K010's five pairs
are untouched, and they still top the list.

Two honest repairs for a later dig are in the case file: compare run-collapsed sequences, or get empirical
per-pair probabilities from the run-preserving null for the longest blocks.
