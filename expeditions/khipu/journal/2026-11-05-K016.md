06:02 PT. Following K015's clue that the published Inkawasi twins share values **in order, with gaps**, I built
the proper instrument: a weighted longest common subsequence (rare numbers count more), written in a few lines of
C so it could compare all 93 528 pairs a hundred times over. The threshold came from the maximum in the shuffled
corpora, and 20 more shuffled corpora were held back to check it.

The verdict is honest and mixed. The tool is calibrated (1 false alarm in 20 held-out corpora), but it is blind
where I most wanted it to see: both Urton & Chu controls fall short, and so do all 50 planted gapped copies. Taking
the global maximum lets the longest khipus set the bar, and small close matches can never clear it. So the published
close matches remain "not testable" for now, and I've written down why.

But one pair cleared the bar by a mile: **KH0097**, in the Musée du Quai Branly in Paris (recorded by the Aschers),
and **KH0586**, in the national museum in Lima (recorded by Hugo Pereyra). Side by side they open the same way, 1080,
12, 22, 100, 40, … 23, 33, and later both lead groups with 1091, 1078 and 1069. Two cords, an ocean apart in their
museum lives, seem to follow one 15-position template. Neither Field Guide page mentions the other, and I found no
paper on it.

I'm keeping my hat on and the champagne corked. It wasn't pre-registered, and the tool failed its controls. So it's a
lead, and the next cycle gets a named, single-pair confirmation test.
