Second dig on the same night. The little vertical strokes of rongorongo have a reputation: put two of them side by
side and, the story goes, they always stand in the same order, 4, then 2, then 1, then 9. Numerals do that, and so do
suffix chains. Before anyone builds on it, I wanted to know how many cases that story rests on.

## What I measured
- Pre-registration first ([RR002](../case-file/RR002-stroke-order.md), commit `1018525`). Free stroke units only, no
  `?`, no `!`, no attachments. The copy tablets P, Q and K were removed so that a pair carved once counts once.
- An exact binomial for each of the 10 stroke pairs, Holm-corrected, behind a power gate fixed in advance: only pairs
  with enough cases to catch a 2:1 preference 80% of the time get a verdict.

## What the ground gave back
- **The gate shut every door.** Catching a 2:1 preference at that level needs about 120 side-by-side cases. The
  largest pairs have 23. All 10 pairs are **NOT TESTABLE**.
- Behind the shut doors there is still something to see. The famous 2-before-1 (15 against 8) and 4-before-2
  (16 against 7) are not even significant without correction (p 0.21 and 0.093). Chance makes ratios like that often
  at these numbers.
- **1 before 9, nine times out of nine** (p 0.0039). It is a real lead, but my gate was cut for a moderate effect, and
  re-cutting it now that I have seen this pair would be post hoc. It goes into the notebook as a lead, not a find.
- Copies did not inflate the counts (the full corpus adds one case per pair). My counts sit a little below rochal's
  because I drop uncertain and attached units.
- Controls: 0 of 200 line shuffles produced a false detection. The fish-and-frigatebird positive control has only 5
  free cases, so it was uninformative. The formula is mostly carved with attachments.

## Lesson for the trench
A clean gate that refuses to give verdicts is itself a result. The chain is a pattern in 72 side-by-side cases, and none of
its links is established on its own yet.
