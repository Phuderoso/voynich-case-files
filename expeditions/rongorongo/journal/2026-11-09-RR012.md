RR010 found something on the three big copies H, P and Q. When two copies disagree about a stroke fused onto a sign,
the copy without it often carves the same stroke loose, right beside the sign. A find from one dig site could be one
workshop's habit, so this morning I went to the only other long pair of copies in the corpus: the Small Santiago
tablet (G) and the Small London tablet (K), which carry the same text. No earlier cycle had touched them.

**The rules went in first** (`e6bc2ac`). The pairing came from the published parallel (Gr1–7 = Kr1–Kv5), not from the
data. Alignment, disagreement, statistic and shuffle were RR010's own code, imported unchanged. This time the power
gate asked the honest question: can the test see *RR010's own effect size* (a 16% excess), not just a huge one? The
p-floor was computed in advance (the RR011 lesson), and the negative control used 40 replicates. Before the push I
counted the trench and smoked the pipeline on a registered shuffle, Kryptos's habit. Both are disclosed in the case file.

**What the trench held.** G and K are faithful copyists. They write the same host sign 100 times, 34 of those with a
stroke on at least one side, and they disagree only **4** times. Four quarrels cannot carry a confirmatory count:
power at RR010's effect size is **0.03**. So the verdict was fixed before the cases were opened: **NOT TESTABLE**
(line 4e), and 4d too (at most 3 informative cases).

**What the cases say, as description only.** In **3 of the 4** disagreements, the bare copy writes the same stroke loose
beside the host. That is every disagreement that *could* score (S1max = 3). The shuffle averages 0.61, and the
descriptive p is 0.009 against a floor of 0.0066. In all three the loose stroke stands on the side where it was fused
(Kr05 `090.001` → Gr04 `090` `001`; Gr06 `290.001` → Kv03 `290` `001.009`; Kv04 `002.450` → Gr07 `002` `450`).
It runs in both directions, K fused and G loose twice, G fused and K loose once. A second script with its own parser
counted the same numbers. It points the same way as RR010, and per case more strongly, but the rules I wrote say a
lead, not a replication, and a lead it stays.
